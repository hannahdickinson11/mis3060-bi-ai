# Part 2 — Business Context Exploration
### Question 1
**Prompt:** Wildcat's data includes credit score, debt-to-income ratio, and annual income, but these measures can tell different stories about the same borrower. If a borrower has a relatively strong credit score but a high debt-to-income ratio, how should Wildcat's risk team interpret that combination in the context of the borrower's income and ability to take on additional debt?

**Summarized Answer:** Credit score, debt-to-income ratio, and annual income each provide different information about a borrower's credit risk. Credit score reflects past borrowing behavior, while DTI shows how much of a borrower's income is already committed to debt. Annual income helps determine how much actual financial flexibility a borrower has, since two borrowers with the same DTI may have very different amounts of income remaining after paying their debts. Therefore, Wildcat should evaluate all three factors together to better assess a borrower's ability to take on additional debt.

**Follow up question raised by response:** If Wildcat finds that borrowers with strong credit scores and high DTI are more vulnerable to financial stress, how should that influence the way Wildcat sets loan approval limits or lending terms for this group?

### Question 2 
**Prompt:** Wildcat's five loan purposes - Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business—represent borrowers using credit for very different economic purposes. If one segment begins experiencing higher delinquency than the others, how should a portfolio committee determine what might be driving that difference and what it should investigate before changing its lending strategy? Focus on strategic decision-making rather than calculating metrics from the dataset.

**Summarized Answer:** If one loan-purpose segment has higher delinquency, the portfolio committee should investigate the underlying cause before changing its lending strategy. The higher delinquency could be caused by borrower characteristics, a specific group of loan vintages, broader economic conditions, or simply temporary noise rather than the loan purpose itself. The committee should also consider the severity of losses and recovery rates, not just how often borrowers become delinquent. Only after identifying the root cause should Wildcat decide whether to change underwriting, adjust lending terms, or limit exposure to that segment.

**Follow up question raised by response:** If a segment has higher delinquency but the issue may actually be driven by borrower characteristics or broader economic conditions, how should Wildcat determine whether it should change its lending strategy for that segment or address the underlying risk factors across the entire portfolio?

### Question 3
**Prompt:** Wildcat's four loan status categories give us different signals about borrower performance. Why is it important for a portfolio committee to distinguish between delinquency and default rather than treating all non-current loans the same? How should the committee think about movement between the four statuses as an early-warning signal for credit risk? Focus on the business implications rather than calculating rates or counts from the dataset.

**Summarized Answer:** Wildcat's four loan statuses should be treated as different stages of credit risk, rather than grouping all non-current loans together. Delinquency is an early sign of financial stress where the borrower may still recover, while default represents a much more serious situation involving likely losses and recovery efforts. The committee should focus not only on how many loans are in each status, but also on how loans move between statuses, including how often delinquent loans return to current versus progress to default. Tracking these transitions gives Wildcat an early-warning system that can help identify deteriorating credit quality before losses become significant.

**Follow up question raised by response:** If movement between loan statuses can provide an early warning of deteriorating credit quality, how should Wildcat use changes in cure rates and roll-to-default rates to decide when it is time to intervene before borrowers reach default?"
